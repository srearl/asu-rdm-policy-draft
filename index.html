challenge: not whether a service exists but whether researchers can find it, choose it, use it well, and connect it to the rest of the data lifecycle.

challenge: ASU lacks a coherent, encompassing strategy that sets norms and expectations for sound data management.

Begin from strength. ASU already has substantial expertise and infrastructure. The strategic gap is the connective tissue that makes distributed capabilities function as an institutional system rather than as a collection the researcher must assemble alone.

proposed mission

Communicate research data management goals and expectations then empower every ASU researcher to steward data responsibly, securely, and sustainably throughout the research data lifecycle.

Through coordinated partnerships, connect people, policy, expertise, and services to advance trustworthy research and discovery of value.

some insights from past RDMO, KE RACI charts: a service needs a clear owner; shared policy requires clear proposal, concurrence, ratification, and escalation rules.

The former shorthand that every decision needs one “A” is too broad. RACI accountability is useful for a defined service deliverable. Institutional policy decisions need a governance pathway that respects mandated jurisdictions and peer authority.

A strategic model is “common outcomes, local expression”: one institutional governance framework that is at the same time encompassing but adaptable to support computational science, human-subjects research, humanities and social sciences, Indigenous and community data, CUI/export-controlled work, classified research, and all of the other diverse research conducted at ASU.

This is not a hub-and-spoke structure. The center is the shared outcome, not the RDM Office. Partners retain authority over staff, budgets, services and regulated responsibilities. The RDM Office provides strategic guidance, convening and coordination of lifecycle interfaces and referrals.

Not every project needs a dedicated data manager but every project does need responsible data leadership!

Embedded and fractional data professionals belong within the training pillar, but they are not merely trainers. They may design workflows, manage data, document decisions, assure quality, curate outputs and broker access to institutional services. A revived graduate RDM course can complement just-in-time and role-based learning.

At the University level, ASU establishes the broader culture and general expectations. At the project level, DMPs reflect those values and become living project records that connect real people, environments, costs, repository choices, and closeout - not a proposal attachment that is forgotten after submission.

Local plans create a feasible baseline for labs and investigators that cannot afford dedicated staff. Project annexes add the requirements that change by sponsor, agreement, discipline and risk. Culture also depends on proposal budgeting, recognition and clear onboarding/offboarding expectations.

infrastructure

ASU excels here, being in rich in physical, digital, and people resources. The challenges are ensuring that researchers are aware of the resources and opportunities available to them, that they have the tools and training to access them, and that we enable seamless transitions across systems as data move through the research data lifecycle.

Do not turn this into a catalog of familiar ASU tools. The strategic point is that infrastructure includes people, platforms and services across the lifecycle. The Navigator helps researchers understand what exists, whether it fits and what must happen before and after its use. Library staff can advise and curate regardless of deposit destination.

The operating principles are (1) reproducibility, and (2) as open as appropriate and as restricted as necessary.

AI and cybersecurity require named leadership, a living control catalog and visible accountability. Their requirements should be implemented through all four pillars. Controlled, export-controlled, CUI or classified research needs fit-for-purpose approved pathways; open-source tools and open data are not universal prescriptions.

Provide support based upon risk, complexity, irreproducibility, lifecycle stage, and equity (not award size alone).

Large grants can budget dedicated staff. Smaller grants may pool fractional capacity. Centrally supported baseline services protect access for small grants, early-career researchers, unfunded scholarship and high-risk projects that cannot independently sustain a specialist.
