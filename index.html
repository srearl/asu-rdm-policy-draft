central concern: ASU is rich in tools and expertise, but lacks a sufficiently visible institutional operating system connecting policy, people, services, project decisions, and lifecycle checkpoints.

Begin from strength. “Operating system” is an organizational metaphor, not a new software platform or reorganization. It means shared outcomes, clear decisions, visible pathways, dependable handoffs, reusable project information, discipline-sensitive assistance, and evidence that the system works.

proposed mission

Enable every ASU researcher to manage data confidently across its full lifecycle by connecting clear expectations, expert guidance, disciplinary practice, and trustworthy infrastructure—making responsible, secure, and reproducible research the easiest path.

responsibleintegrity · ethics · appropriate openness · reproducibility

researcher north star: I know what is expected, where to go, and what to do next.

This is proposed language for discussion, not approved institutional language. It draws on ASU’s commitment to research and discovery of public value and on the collaborative, campus-wide service orientation used by Cornell Data Services. Science: A New Golden Age is not an RDM report and does not itself impose award terms on ASU; it is a policy signal.

A strategic model is “common outcomes, local expression”: one institutional governance framework that is at the same time encompassing but adaptable to support computational science, human-subjects research, humanities and social sciences, etc., etc.

This is not a hub-and-spoke structure. The center is the shared outcome, not the RDM Office. Partners retain authority over staff, budgets, services and regulated responsibilities. The RDM Office provides strategic guidance, convening and coordination of lifecycle interfaces and referrals.

The Office owns the connective work and evidence that the institutional program works. It does not own Library repositories or curation; technology platforms; cybersecurity approvals or incident response; IRB, HIPAA, privacy, legal, contract, or export-control decisions; clinical-partner data; or faculty scientific methods. “No wrong door” does not mean one compulsory door. If the Office incubates a missing service, the pilot should have an explicit duration, evaluation, and durable owner or exit.

Not every project needs a dedicated data manager but every project does need responsible data leadership!

Ideally, support should scale with risk, complexity, lifecycle stage, equity, not award size alone.

Embedded and fractional data professionals belong within the training pillar, but they are not merely trainers. They may design workflows, manage data, document decisions, assure quality, curate outputs, and broker access to institutional services. The prior ASU graduate RDM course can complement just-in-time and role-based learning.

projectliving project recordsponsor, ethics, agreement, security, cost, and repository specifics

Culture shifts when ASU makes stewardship expected, supported, visible, and rewarded.

Small projects must be able to capitalize on shared capacity rather than being held to unfunded mandates.

At the University level, ASU establishes the broader culture and general expectations. At the levels of project, labs, and investigators, DMPs and tailored data policies reflect ASU’s values and become living project records that connect real people, environments, costs, repository choices, and closeout.

Investigators should plan data-management, curation, repository, security, and preservation costs in future grants when allowable. Larger awards can support dedicated professionals; smaller projects need pooled or subsidized capacity. Recognition should include datasets, software, documentation, curation, reproducibility, and data-professional contributions.

infrastructure

ASU excels here, being rich in physical, digital, and people resources. The challenges are ensuring that researchers are aware of the resources and opportunities available to them, that they have the tools and training to access them, and that we enable seamless transitions across systems as data move through the research data lifecycle.

Do not turn this into a catalog of familiar ASU tools. The strategic point is that infrastructure includes people, platforms and services across the lifecycle. The Navigator helps researchers understand what exists, whether it fits and what must happen before and after its use. Library staff can advise and curate regardless of deposit destination.

The operating principles are (1) reproducibility, and (2) as open as appropriate and as restricted as necessary.

We might consider the 2026 OSTP report a policy signal, and that RDM should provide verification and AI readiness, not just grant compliance.

AI and cybersecurity require named leadership, a living control catalog and visible accountability. Their requirements should be implemented through all four pillars. Controlled, export-controlled, CUI or classified research needs fit-for-purpose approved pathways; open-source tools and open data are not universal prescriptions.

These are selected differentiators, not rankings or complete institutional profiles. Each model reflects local structures and should be adapted rather than copied.
